# Career decision frameworks

Read only the sections relevant to the current request. These are decision aids, not scoring rituals.

## 1. Career capital map

Map five assets before recommending a major move:

1. **Transferable capabilities** — Skills that remain valuable across teams, companies, or industries.
2. **Proof of impact** — Outcomes, work samples, metrics, and stories that make capability credible.
3. **Relationships** — Peers, mentors, sponsors, customers, and communities that create information or opportunity.
4. **Reputation and distribution** — Who knows the user's work and what they reliably associate with it.
5. **Runway and options** — Time, money, energy, credentials, location flexibility, and fallback choices.

Find the asset that is both weak and pivotal. Improving that asset may be safer than changing jobs immediately.

## 2. Define the real decision

Rewrite vague questions into a decision sentence:

```text
By [date], decide whether to [option A] or [option B] in order to achieve [outcome], while respecting [non-negotiable constraints].
```

Then ask:

- What happens if no decision is made for three months?
- Is this a one-way door or a two-way door?
- Which uncertainty, if resolved, would most change the choice?

## 3. Option scorecard

Use a scorecard only when there are several concrete options. Agree on criteria before scoring.

| Criterion | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Role fit | Daily work, strengths used, autonomy, meaning |
| Learning and trajectory | Skill growth, scope, future option value |
| Manager and environment | Manager quality, team health, decision rights |
| Economics | Total compensation, stability, benefits, equity realism |
| Sustainability | Hours, energy, commute, health, family constraints |
| Reversibility | Ease of returning, transferable signal, downside containment |
| Confidence | Amount and quality of evidence behind the score |

Use weights only for priorities the user has explicitly ranked. A weighted total is a conversation aid, not objective truth. Always show:

- the recommendation;
- the two criteria driving it;
- the largest uncertainty;
- the evidence that would reverse it.

## 4. Push, pull, and bridge for transitions

Separate three forces:

- **Push:** What is unhealthy or exhausted in the current situation?
- **Pull:** What specific work, environment, or identity is genuinely attractive?
- **Bridge:** What evidence, skill, relationship, or runway makes the move plausible?

Strong push with weak pull often calls for recovery or a better local environment before a full career switch. Strong pull with a missing bridge calls for an experiment and capability plan.

## 5. Reversible career experiment

A useful experiment tests one important assumption without pretending to simulate the entire career.

```text
Hypothesis: I will enjoy and perform well in [target work].
Critical assumption: [one thing that must be true].
Action: [realistic work sample, shadowing, interview, project, or application sprint].
Time box: [usually 1–4 weeks].
Evidence: [observable behavior or external feedback].
Decision rule: If [signal], continue; if [signal], revise or stop.
Cost cap: [time, money, reputation, or energy limit].
```

Prefer contact with the real work over more abstract reflection.

## 6. Promotion evidence map

Translate “I want a promotion” into evidence expected at the next level:

- outcomes and scope already demonstrated;
- behaviors expected but not yet consistently visible;
- decision-makers and sponsors who need evidence;
- one stretch responsibility that produces relevant proof;
- feedback cadence and a date for an explicit readiness conversation.

Do not assume excellent execution alone creates promotion. Visibility, sponsorship, organizational need, and timing may matter; state that candidly.

## 7. Offer and company diligence

Before recommending an Offer, gather evidence about:

- the manager's expectations, coaching behavior, and reason the role is open;
- what success looks like at 3, 6, and 12 months;
- team turnover, decision process, workload, and current challenges;
- compensation details, contingencies, and realistic equity assumptions;
- what skills and signals the role creates for the next move;
- the downside scenario and available fallback.

Treat interview impressions as evidence of limited quality. Seek behavioral examples and independent signals.

## 8. Recommendation confidence

Label confidence when stakes are high:

- **High:** The decisive facts are known and consistent.
- **Medium:** A direction is reasonable, but one or two unknowns could change it.
- **Low:** The recommendation is mainly a hypothesis; run an experiment before committing.

Never inflate confidence to sound helpful.
